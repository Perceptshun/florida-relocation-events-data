02 — The three rooms

What changes in each photo

Room one · Fire pit lounge

A gravel circle under the tree, and the tree never moves

This leg is the shadiest part of the yard by mid-afternoon, which is exactly why the grass gave up here. Stop fighting it. A 16-foot circle of gold crushed granite, compacted and edged, sits partly under the canopy so the tree is doing the work an umbrella would.

  • Fire pit — three courses of trapezoid wall block dry-stacked into a 36" ring with a 30" steel insert and red lava rock. Roughly $155 and two hours.
  • Mulch ring — 6' of brown bark around the trunk, pulled 4" back off the bark itself so you don't rot the collar.
  • Firebush along the left fence — scarlet flowers, hummingbirds, and leaves that actually turn red here in November.
  • Keep the gravel strip already running along the house; just top it up with matching river rock.
  • Crushed granite, 3" deep over 200 sq ft2 tons
  • Fire pit clearance to fence and trunk12 ft min
Room two · Terrace

Extend the slab you already have instead of breaking it

Demo is the single most expensive thing you could do here and it buys nothing. The slab is sound and roughly level, so lay tumbled pavers on a compacted base right up to its edges, flush, with a soldier course framing the whole terrace. Read from the window it becomes one surface.

  • Sierra / autumn blend tumbled pavers in a running bond — the terracotta, tan and charcoal mix is the fall palette straight out of the pallet.
  • Keep the bird of paradise. They're already the right backdrop; add five muhly grass in front so October gives you a pink haze at eye level.
  • Two crushed-granite paths leave the terrace — one curving left to the fire circle, one running straight back to the kitchen.
  • Your existing black table and chairs stay. Iron black is already in the palette; don't buy furniture.
  • New paver area around the slab280 sq ft
  • Base build-up under new pavers4" + 1" sand
Room three · Outdoor kitchen

The corner the dining room stares at

Right now this corner is the weakest thing in the yard and it is the first thing the window sees. An L-shaped counter wrapping the back and right fence turns it into the focal point — and the hose bib and exterior outlet already on that wall mean you get water and power without a trench.

  • Block-and-slab construction — dry-stacked CMU set with masonry adhesive, no mortar joints, no footing inspection, no mason.
  • German-smear finish over the block with a bag of mortar and a sponge, so the counter matches the white block house instead of fighting it.
  • Corrugated galvanized backsplash behind the grill bay — a farm detail that happens to be the heat shield the vinyl fence needs.
  • Bar ledge with three stools on the right run, facing back toward the fire pit so the two ends of the yard talk to each other.

03 — The kitchen

Block and slab, under a thousand dollars

No mortar, no footing, no contractor. Two courses of dry-stacked block, a slab top, and a finish that reads far more expensive than it is.

How it goes together

Five moves

  • Level a base. 4" of compacted paver base under the whole counter footprint, screeded flat. This is the only step that matters — everything above it is forgiving.
  • Dry-stack two courses. Block laid cores-down, each course glued to the one below with construction adhesive. Leave a 32" open bay for the grill and a 16" bay under the sink.
  • Cap it. 24×24 slabs bedded in adhesive with a 1" overhang all around. On the right run, run the PT bar top 12" proud of the face for knees.
  • Smear it. Wet the block, trowel on thinned mortar, wipe back with a damp sponge so block texture shows through. One afternoon, and it ties the counter to the house wall.
  • Shield and plumb. Corrugated panel on 2×4 furring behind the grill bay, 6" off the fence. Bar sink drains through a hose to a gravel dry well; feed it off the existing hose bib with a quick-connect you disconnect in January.

Do not skip the air gap. Vinyl fence panels start to soften and cup somewhere around 160 °F, and a closed grill hood radiating at a panel 4 inches away will get there on a July afternoon. The galvanized panel plus 6 inches of moving air is the whole fix, and it costs $92.

07 — Sequence

Four weekends, in this order

The order matters: base work before anything pretty, and the kitchen before the surfaces around it so you can drop block without protecting pavers.

Weekend one

Clear and shape

  • Call 811 — there's irrigation out there
  • Spray or sheet-mulch the remaining turf
  • Mark all three zones with paint and a string line
  • Grade away from the house, ¼" per foot
Weekend two

Build the kitchen

  • Base and compact the counter footprint
  • Dry-stack both courses, leave the bays
  • Set the slab top and the bar board
  • Furring, heat shield, smear finish
Weekend three

Lay the hard surfaces

  • Base, screed, and set terrace pavers
  • Cut and set the soldier course border
  • Edge and compact both granite paths
  • Sweep polymeric sand, mist, done
Weekend four

Circle, rock, plants

  • Granite circle and the fire pit ring
  • Fabric and river rock along the house
  • Set plants, then mulch around them
  • Run the low-voltage line and lights
08 — Before you start

Florida fine print

Termites

Keep bark mulch a full 12–18" off the block walls. The river rock drip strip in the plan is doing that job, not decoration — and it's why the existing gravel strip along the left wall should stay.

Fabric

Woven landscape fabric under rock, yes. Under mulch, never — it fails in about two seasons and then you're pulling shredded plastic out of the bed. Use plain cardboard and 3" of mulch instead.

Drainage

Your yard already sheds toward the back fence. Keep that. Pavers and compacted granite both shed water, so removing turf means slightly more runoff — hold ¼" per foot of fall away from the slab and don't dam the low corner with edging.

Fire pit

Wood-burning pits need 12 feet of clearance to the fence, the house and the tree canopy, and many Central Florida HOAs and counties restrict open burning during dry season. A propane insert sidesteps all of it for about $320 more.

HOA

Check the ARC rules before you set block. Permanent structures along a fence line and anything over 30" tall are the two things that commonly need approval, and a dry-stacked counter still reads as permanent.

Weeds

No grass doesn't mean no weeding. Two applications of a granular pre-emergent a year, spring and fall, over rock and mulch alike, is the difference between this looking sharp in year three and looking abandoned.
